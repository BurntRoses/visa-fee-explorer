Minister’s message

I am pleased to present Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada’s (IRCC) report on fees for 2025–26.

The Service Fees Act provides a modern legislative framework that enables cost-effective delivery of services and, through better reporting to Parliament, improves transparency and oversight.

As with previous reports, and in the spirit of open and transparent fee management, we have included a breakdown of individual fees under IRCC’s authority, along with anticipated adjustments, service standards and performance results.

IRCC has over 70 user fees established in the Regulations pursuant to the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, the Citizenship Act and the Financial Administration Act. While the majority of IRCC’s fees are service fees, the department also charges some regulatory fees which grant a right or a privilege, or support the regulation of a fee scheme. IRCC charges fees in relation to applications for Temporary Residence, Permanent Residence, Citizenship, Passport and other travel documents, and other immigration-related services.

In 2025–26, IRCC saw a decrease in fee revenue compared to the previous fiscal year. This is mainly explained by the ongoing impact of measures introduced to manage temporary resident volumes, including caps and program adjustments for international students and temporary workers, as well as continued reductions to permanent resident admission targets. These measures are intended to bring immigration back to sustainable levels, ultimately strengthening Canada’s economy by meeting long-term labour market and demographic needs, while aligning immigration targets with community capacity. Decreasing fee revenue can also be attributed to various global events, such as the rising cost of living and reductions in global travel. IRCC continues to work closely with partners to attract and retain global talent to support Canada’s long-term prosperity, while also maintaining support of those facing humanitarian crises.

I welcome the increased transparency and oversight that the Service Fees Act’s reporting regime embodies.

The Honourable Lena Metlege Diab, ECNS, KC, P.C., M.P.
Minister of Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship

About this report

This report, which is tabled under section 20 of the Service Fees Act, the Low-materiality Fees Regulations, and subsection 4.2.9 of the Treasury Board Directive on Charging and Special Financial Authorities, contains information about the fees IRCC had the authority to charge in fiscal year 2025–26.

The report covers fees that are subject to the Service Fees Act and exempted from the Service Fees Act.

For reporting purposes, fees are categorized by fee-setting mechanism. There are three mechanisms:

  1. Act

    The authority to set these fees is delegated to a department, minister or Governor in Council pursuant to an Act of Parliament. This category naturally includes fees listed in regulations and in fees notices.

  2. Contract

    Most ministers have the authority to enter into contracts, which are usually negotiated between the minister and an individual or organization, and which cover fees and other terms and conditions. In some cases, that authority may also be provided by an Act of Parliament.

  3. Market rate or auction

    The authority to set these fees comes from an Act of Parliament or a regulation, and the minister, department or Governor in Council has no control in determining the fee amount.

For fees set by Act, the report provides totals for fee groupings, as well as detailed information for each fee. IRCC did not have fees set by contract, market rate or auction.

Fees charged by IRCC under the Access to Information Act are not subject to the Service Fees Act and are not included in this report. Details on access to information fees can be found in IRCC’s Annual Report on the Access to Information Act.

Remissions

Service Fees Act authority

In 2025–26, IRCC was subject to the requirements to issue remissions under section 7 of the Service Fees Act and subsection 4.2.4 of the Treasury Board Directive on Charging and Special Financial Authorities to remit a fee, in whole or in part, to a fee payer when a service standard was not met. IRCC’s remission policy and procedures, pursuant to the Service Fees Act, are on the following web page: Departmental Remission Policy Pursuant to the Service Fees Act.

Financial Administration Act authority

In 2025–26, IRCC obtained remissions from the Governor in Council under the Financial Administration Act, many of which related to the issuance of public policies (past and present). IRCC completed two Omnibus Remission Orders in 2025–26, accounting for fees owing to the Crown that were not collected or will not be collected, or that will be reimbursed. In accordance with TBS guidance, IRCC is committed to seeking remission orders in advance whenever possible. Where advance approval is not possible, such as in crisis response situations, IRCC will seek remissions within three months of a decision.

For a list of remissions issued in 2025–26, refer to Annex A: Remissions issued under the Financial Administration Act in 2025–26.

Enabling Legislation

In 2025–26, IRCC also issued remissions under its enabling legislation. These remissions were for reasons other than not meeting a service standard.

The regulation-making authority to remit is set out in the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act and is detailed in the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations.

A fee grouping is a set of fees relating to a single business line, directorate or program that a department had the authority to charge for those activities.

This section presents, for each fee grouping, the total revenue, costs, and remissions related to missed service standards for all fees IRCC had the authority to charge in 2025–26 that are set by Act. This includes all fees charged by the department, as IRCC did not have fees set by contract, market rate or auction.

The Citizenship Program administers citizenship legislation and promotes the rights and responsibilities of Canadian citizenship. IRCC administers the acquisition of Canadian citizenship by developing, implementing, and applying legislation, regulations and policies that protect the integrity of Canadian citizenship and allow eligible applicants to be granted citizenship or be provided with a proof of citizenship.

  • Annual Adjustments: Only the Right of Citizenship Fee is adjusted annually on March 31st based on the percentage change in the Consumer Price Index, pursuant to section 17 of the Service Fees Act.

Passport Program Fees

The Passport Program provides an essential service to Canadians, supporting each citizen’s right to enter and leave Canada under the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms. IRCC, in collaboration with its service delivery partners, Employment and Social Development Canada/Service Canada (ESDC/SC) and Global Affairs Canada (GAC), delivers Canada’s Passport Program. The Passport Program is responsible for issuing a range of passports and other travel documents to facilitate travel outside of Canada, including regular and temporary passports and emergency travel documents for Canadian citizens, diplomatic and special passports for official government travellers, refugee travel documents for protected persons residing in Canada, and certificates of identity for permanent residents who are unable to obtain a passport from another country.

Permanent Residence Program Fees

Foreign nationals can immigrate to Canada permanently through a variety of pathways and programs. These permanent resident programs grant individuals the right to live, work or study anywhere in the country. IRCC facilitates the entry of permanent residents in a way that maximizes their economic, social, and cultural contributions to Canada, while also safeguarding the health, safety and security of all Canadians.

  • Fee-setting authority: Immigration and Refugee Protection Act; Financial Administration Act (Right of Permanent Residence Fee only)
  • Bi-annual Adjustments: All Permanent Residence Program Fees, with the exception of the Renewal or replacement of a Permanent Resident Card (PRC) fee and the Permanent Resident Travel Document fee, are adjusted bi-annually on April 30th. These fees are adjusted every two years based on the cumulative percentage change to the Consumer Price Index for the previous two years, pursuant to subsection 303(1.1) and section 294 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations. The next Permanent Residence Program fee adjustment will occur on April 30th, 2028.

Temporary Residence Program Fees

Temporary resident status is granted when an individual meets the legislative requirements to enter or remain in Canada as a visitor, student, worker, or holder of a temporary resident permit. Only foreign nationals who are physically present in Canada hold temporary resident status. One of the objectives of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act is to facilitate the entry of visitors, students and temporary workers for purposes such as trade, commerce, tourism, international understanding and cultural, educational and scientific activities.

  • Fee-setting authority: Immigration and Refugee Protection Act; Financial Administration Act (International Experience Canada Fee only)
  • Annual Adjustments: The following fees are adjusted annually on December 1st based on the percentage change in the Consumer Price Index, pursuant to section 17 of the Service Fees Act:
    • International Experience Canada Fee
    • Temporary Resident Permit
    • Restoration of Temporary Resident Status
